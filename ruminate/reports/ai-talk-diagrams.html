How a Context Window Works

Every AI model has a hard limit on how much text it can hold at once — like a camera viewfinder sliding over a document. When conversation grows beyond the window, older content falls out and the model loses access to it entirely.

Conversation: 0 / 16 blocks  |  Context window: 8 blocks

Click + Add Message to watch early messages fall out of the context window. The model only sees what’s inside the frame.

Scale: What fits in a context window?

Equivalences use ~750 words per page and ~5 characters per token as rough guides.

Diagram 3 of 5

Context Window Degradation

A full context window doesn’t just slow down — it causes answer quality to collapse. Models work well under 70–80% full. Beyond that, a sharp cliff: repetition, lost instructions, subtle errors.

Context at 70% full
70%
Answer quality: High
“Here’s a concise summary of the three key risks you asked about. First, the vendor lock-in issue you mentioned at the start of our conversation …”
Context at 95% full
95%
Answer quality: Degraded
“As I mentioned, there are several risks. Vendor lock-in is one. There are also risks related to… vendor lock-in. Additionally, you should consider vendor lock-in…”

The degradation curve is illustrative — exact behaviour varies by model, task type, and where key instructions appear. The cliff effect is well-documented across frontier models.

Training vs. Inference

Training is building the factory — expensive, slow, done once. Inference is running the factory — fast, cheap by comparison, millions of times per day.

Phase 1
Training
Cost
$10M – $1B+ per run
Duration
Weeks to months
Compute
Tens of thousands of GPUs
Frequency
Done once (or rarely)
The model sees trillions of tokens of text and adjusts billions of parameters to minimise prediction errors. When training ends, those numbers are frozen — the “weights file.”
Phase 2
Inference
Cost
Fractions of a cent per query
Duration
Milliseconds to seconds
Compute
A handful of GPUs
Frequency
Millions of times per day
You send your prompt. The frozen model runs it through its parameter layers and predicts the next token — over and over until the answer is complete. It doesn’t learn from your query.

Cost figures are approximate. GPT-4-class training costs are estimated at $50M–$100M per run; Llama-class open models are cheaper by one to two orders of magnitude.

What is a Parameter?

A parameter is a single learned number — a connection strength between neurons. Every wire in the network has a weight. That weight is a parameter. GPT-4-class models have roughly a trillion of them, all frozen after training.

The scale of modern models

★ = publicly confirmed. MoE (Mixture-of-Experts) models have a large total parameter count but only activate a fraction per query — so compute cost is much lower than the headline number suggests.
