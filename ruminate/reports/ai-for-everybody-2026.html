AI For Everybody 2026

A comprehensive primer for navigating the AI transformation — what's real, what's hype, what's genuinely astonishing, what's legitimately concerning, and what you can actually do about it. For the curious person who's heard bits and pieces and needs a coherent picture.

Published April 30, 2026
Author Kishbrac
Read Time 50 min
Coverage Period 2022–April 2026
Summary

The moment we're in

Something shifted in AI around 2022 that distinguishes this moment from every previous wave of AI hype. Prior generations of AI were narrow: they played chess, recognized faces, recommended videos. Current AI systems are broad — they write, reason, code, translate, diagnose, create, and act. Not perfectly. Not without supervision. But at a level of general capability that is genuinely new in the history of computing.

By April 2026, global investment in AI has reached $202.3 billion — 50% of all venture capital deployed worldwide. More than 50% of new code commits on GitHub involve AI assistance. The most powerful AI systems are being built by fewer than a dozen organizations worldwide, and seven of them collectively touch billions of daily users. A student with internet access anywhere on earth and a paralegal at a New York law firm are both navigating the same technology, with very different stakes.

This report does not pretend to predict the future. The last three years have repeatedly surprised people who claimed to know exactly where AI was going. What it offers instead: a grounded understanding of what's actually happening now, a calibrated sense of where the real risks and real opportunities lie, and specific frameworks for navigating this moment — whether you're a parent worried about your children's futures, a worker wondering about your career, a retiree trying to protect yourself from new scams, or a citizen trying to understand what's being built and by whom.

Section 1 — How We Got Here

The timeline: from novelty to infrastructure

The story of AI's transformation isn't a single breakthrough — it's a compounding sequence. Each development made the next feel inevitable, and the cumulative effect is a shift from AI as a curiosity to AI as infrastructure. Understanding the arc helps make sense of why right now feels different.

Nov 2022
ChatGPT launches — 100 million users in 60 days

Faster consumer adoption than smartphones, social media, or the internet itself. The key wasn't a capability breakthrough — it was accessibility. Plain English, instant answers, no manual required. A billion-dollar technology became a free webpage.

2023
The year everyone noticed — images, code, voice

Midjourney, DALL-E, and Stable Diffusion went mainstream for images. GitHub Copilot crossed 1 million paid subscribers. Voice synthesis moved past the uncanny valley. GPT-4 passed the bar exam in the 90th percentile. The phrase "AI-generated" entered everyday language.

Early 2024
Intelligence inflection — Claude 3, Gemini 1.5, context windows explode

Claude 3 Opus scored above human average on the bar exam, medical licensing, and GRE benchmarks. Gemini 1.5 introduced a 1-million token context window — you could feed it the entire Harry Potter series in one conversation. For the first time, AI was reliably better than most people at specific cognitive tasks.

May 2024
GPT-4o — the multimodal moment

Seamless voice, vision, and text in one real-time conversation. Natural language became the universal interface to computation. The scenario from the 2013 film Her — an AI that talks, listens, and responds naturally — went from science fiction to product demo.

Oct 2024
Reasoning models — AI starts "thinking out loud"

OpenAI's o1 introduces chain-of-thought reasoning: the model "thinks through" problems before answering. First reliable PhD-level performance on hard mathematics and science. The dismissal of AI as "just autocomplete" became difficult to defend with a straight face.

Jan 2025
DeepSeek R1 — the rules change overnight

A Chinese AI lab releases an open-source reasoning model matching OpenAI's o1 on benchmarks, reportedly built at a fraction of the cost. NVIDIA stock drops 17% in a single day — $600 billion in market capitalization erased. The assumption that only trillion-dollar companies could build frontier AI collapses in 24 hours.

2025
The Agency Year — AI stops chatting and starts doing

Agentic AI: systems that take multi-step actions toward goals, not just generate text. AI browsing websites, writing and running code, sending emails, managing workflows — autonomously. AI-assisted code exceeds 50% of new GitHub commits. 79% of enterprises report adopting agents; only 11% run them reliably in production — the gap between demo and deployment in real time.

Apr 2026
Normalization — AI as standard infrastructure

The most intense month in AI model history: Claude Opus 4.7 (April 16), GPT-5.5 (April 23), DeepSeek V4 Preview (April 24) all in two weeks. For knowledge workers, AI assistance is as standard as spell-check was in 2005. The question shifts from "will this matter?" to "how do we live with this well?"

"We are not in a moment of AI hype followed by an AI winter. We are in the early years of an infrastructure change comparable to electrification — one that will take decades to fully play out and is already irreversible."

Section 2 — What AI Actually Does

Real capabilities, honest limitations

The biggest source of public confusion about AI is the gap between marketing narratives and operational reality. Both uncritical enthusiasm and dismissive skepticism miss the truth. A calibrated map of what's real matters more than any headline.

What AI does reliably well

Capability 01
Writing, editing, summarizing, translating — at professional quality
Professional-quality drafting across business, legal, medical, and technical contexts. Real-time translation at professional quality across 100+ languages. Document summarization with high accuracy. These are the AI applications already deployed at scale, generating documented productivity gains, and available free to anyone with internet access.
Capability 02
Code generation — the most transformative near-term application
Developers using AI tools report 55% faster task completion. GitHub Copilot has 4.7 million paid subscribers (January 2026, up 75% year over year) and is used by 90% of Fortune 100 companies. AI-assisted code now exceeds 50% of new GitHub commits. You still need to understand what you're building — AI eliminates much of the typing, not the thinking.
Capability 03
Analysis and synthesis from provided materials
Given source documents, AI can synthesize information, identify key arguments, produce structured analyses, and answer specific questions with high accuracy. This is "AI as brilliant research assistant" — most effective when working from material you've provided, not from AI's training memory alone. The distinction matters enormously for avoiding errors.
Capability 04
Accessible explanation — perhaps the most democratizing capability
AI can explain anything clearly at whatever level you need. Medical jargon in a doctor's letter. Legal contract language. Technical product specifications. The information was always there; AI makes it accessible without an expensive human interpreter. A person navigating a complex health diagnosis alone now has access to something that functions like an informed, patient explainer — free, available at 3am, never condescending.

The hallucination problem — the most important limitation to understand

AI language models can and do generate plausible-sounding but factually wrong information — confidently and fluently. Not sometimes. Routinely. The technical reason: AI generates the most statistically probable continuation of text given its context. When it doesn't know something, it doesn't say "I don't know" — it generates something that reads like an answer, with the same style and confidence as when it's correct.

Across 37 models tested in 2026 benchmarks, hallucination rates range from 15% to 52% depending on model and task. Medical case summaries show 64% hallucination rates without mitigation. Legal queries: 69–88% on specific questions. Models are 34% more likely to use confident language when generating incorrect information — the opposite of what you'd want.

Critical Caution

Never use AI output as a sole source for factual claims that matter: medical information, legal specifics, financial numbers, historical facts, quotes attributed to real people. Ask AI to provide sources — then verify those sources actually exist and say what the AI claims. Many don't. The AI will sound certain even when it's fabricating.

The most accurate mental model: AI is like a brilliant, tireless, encyclopedic intern who sometimes makes things up confidently. Enormous output volume, impressive synthesis, available at 3am. But they need supervision, will occasionally produce authoritative-sounding nonsense, and for anything truly consequential, you want someone who actually knows the domain to review their work.

Section 3 — The Amazing

Real breakthroughs worth celebrating

It is easy for AI discourse to skew toward anxiety. But intellectual honesty requires accounting for genuine, measurable improvements to human life — many arriving right now, in ways that don't always make headlines. These are peer-reviewed, clinically validated, economically documented gains.

Medicine: the protein folding revolution

For 50 years, determining a single protein's 3D structure — the shape that determines its biological function — could take a PhD student an entire dissertation. There are approximately 200 million known proteins. DeepMind's AlphaFold essentially solved this problem: AlphaFold 3 (2024) predicts the structure of all biological molecules — proteins, DNA, RNA, small molecules — in minutes with high accuracy. The AlphaFold database has been downloaded by 2+ million researchers in 190+ countries.

Drug discovery timelines are compressing from 5–10 years to potentially 1–3 years. Multiple AI-discovered drug candidates entered Phase 2 clinical trials in 2025. Eli Lilly committed up to $2.75 billion in a deal with AI-driven drug discovery firm Insilico Medicine. AI identified promising antibiotic candidates capable of eliminating MRSA — antibiotic-resistant staph — from a screen of 36 million molecules. No new antibiotic class had been discovered since 1987. AI found candidates in months.

Medical imaging: catching what human attention misses

Multiple peer-reviewed studies show AI performing at or above specialist radiologist level for specific imaging tasks: breast cancer detection, diabetic retinopathy screening (deployed at scale in India, Thailand, and the UK), and lung cancer CT screening. EchoNext's AI system outperformed cardiologists detecting heart disease from ECGs, trained on over one million heart scans. The FDA has approved over 800 AI/ML medical devices.

The best outcome in every case is human+AI: augmented physicians outperform either alone. In regions with severe specialist shortages — which is most of the world — AI is expanding access that would otherwise not exist.

The democratization of expertise

Consider what was previously expensive or inaccessible, now available to anyone with internet access:

Access Story
The crop disease app with 1 million users
A farmer in rural Kenya photographs a diseased cassava plant with a basic Android phone. An AI app identifies the disease and provides treatment recommendations — in Swahili, free. One agricultural extension officer might serve 5,000 farmers. This app serves every farmer with a smartphone. Over 1 million users. Versions now cover 12 crops and 40+ diseases across Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia.

The special education appeal that succeeded
A single mother working two jobs needed to appeal a school district decision affecting her daughter's special education services. She couldn't afford an attorney. Using a free AI assistant, she drafted a formal appeal with correct legal citations, proper structure, and the right procedural arguments. Wealthy families hire consultants at $150–300/hour for this. The appeal succeeded. This scenario is now playing out millions of times for tenants facing eviction, workers denied unemployment, families navigating disability applications.
Access Story
The diagnostic odyssey shortened
Children with rare diseases average 4–7 years to diagnosis in the US, often seeing 7+ specialists first. AI pattern recognition is beginning to change this: cross-referencing symptom combinations, lab values, and genetic markers across records no single physician has synthesized. Multiple documented cases in 2025 of AI identifying rare conditions — Mitochondrial Disease, specific connective tissue disorders — that had been missed for years. Not every case succeeds. But the direction of travel is unmistakable.

Science and climate acceleration

Google DeepMind's GraphCast weather forecasting model outperforms the leading European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts on key metrics, and is measurably better than traditional models at hurricane intensity prediction. AI identified 2.2 million new crystal structures — including 380,000 predicted stable materials — more than the sum of all documented materials in human scientific history. Real-time captioning for the deaf community has crossed the 95% accuracy threshold in controlled conditions. Climate modeling, population genomics, and materials science are all being transformed at a pace that will compound for decades.

Section 4 — The Concerning

Real risks, honestly addressed

Honest concern is not panic. These are real problems — many well-documented, some still speculative. For each: what's actually happening, what evidence supports it, what remains contested, and what can be done.

1. Job displacement: the counterintuitive reality

AI is disrupting white-collar cognitive work first — reversing 200 years of automation history. Previous waves automated physical labor and gave rise to the knowledge economy. This wave targets entry-level writing, paralegal work, basic data analysis, customer service, graphic design production, coding scaffolding, and similar cognitive-routine tasks. The World Economic Forum projects 92 million roles displaced by 2030 and 170 million new ones created — a net gain of 78 million jobs. But the new jobs cluster in different sectors, require different skills, and concentrate in different geographies than the displaced ones.

Real data from 2025: 77,999 AI-attributed tech job losses in the first half of 2025. Software developer employment for those aged 22–25 fell 20% from its late-2022 peak. The specific cohort most affected: entry-level cognitive workers in large organizations — the same group that has historically used those entry positions to build experience and advance.

The Gender Dimension

The displacement is not demographically neutral. 79% of employed US women work in high-automation-risk jobs versus 58% of men. 58.87 million women hold high-exposure positions versus 48.62 million men. 4.7% of women's jobs face severe disruption potential versus 2.4% for men. The women most at risk are concentrated in clerical, administrative, customer service, and data-entry roles — the entry points that previous generations used to enter the middle class. Without targeted intervention, AI-driven displacement will compound existing gender economic gaps.

The Distribution Problem

The most important and under-discussed dimension: productivity gains from automation don't automatically distribute broadly. History shows those gains flowing primarily to capital owners and highly skilled workers. McKinsey estimates today's AI technology could automate ~57% of current US work hours — not 57% of jobs, but hours within jobs. The worker whose tasks are automated needs new tasks or a new job. The company captured the efficiency value. Without policy intervention — retraining programs, worker protection, taxation of AI-generated productivity — this pattern repeats.

2. Misinformation and AI fraud at scale

8 million deepfakes are projected to have been shared in 2025 — up from 500,000 in 2023, a 16x increase in two years. AI-powered scams surged 1,210% in 2025. The most alarming specific case: a finance employee at international firm Arup transferred $25.6 million in 15 transactions after a video call where the entire "team" — including the CFO — were AI-generated deepfakes. The FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report documented $893 million in AI-related scam losses. Deepfake-enabled fraud is projected to reach $40 billion in global losses by 2027.

Voice cloning is particularly dangerous for ordinary people: scammers can generate convincing audio from as little as 3 seconds of voice — easily obtained from social media. "Grandparent scams" now use cloned voices of grandchildren in fake distress emergencies. One in three people who engage with AI-powered scam calls lose money; the average loss is $18,000.

The "liar's dividend" compounds the problem: the mere existence of deepfakes has given defendants a new strategy — claim authentic evidence against them is AI-generated. Even when disproved, the defense introduces reasonable doubt. The deepfake problem runs in both directions.

3. The power concentration problem

Seven organizations control the frontier AI systems that most people on earth use. In 2025, OpenAI and Anthropic together captured 14% of all global venture capital investment across all sectors. NVIDIA — which supplies the chips most AI training runs on — holds 90%+ market share in cloud AI accelerators and posted FY2025 revenue of $130.5 billion, up 114% year over year. In Q3 2025, 61% of NVIDIA's revenue came from just four companies.

Individual billionaires — by way of their companies — currently have more direct influence over the trajectory of AI development than any government or democratic process. This is not an argument that any individual has bad intentions. It is a structural observation about who is making decisions that affect everyone, with limited accountability to anyone except shareholders.

4. The regulation gap

The EU AI Act (in force August 2024, enforcement phased 2025–2026) is the world's most serious AI regulation — a risk-tiered framework with real penalties up to €35 million or 7% of global revenue. It bans AI-powered social scoring, real-time public biometric surveillance, and manipulation of vulnerable groups. It requires human oversight and bias testing for "high-risk" AI in hiring, healthcare, education, and legal decisions.

The US has no comprehensive federal AI law as of April 2026. The Biden-era AI Executive Order (October 2023) was revoked by the Trump administration in January 2025. Colorado's Anti-Discrimination in AI Law (effective February 2026) is the only state-level comprehensive AI law in effect. The most consequential decisions about training data, safety testing, and deployment remain largely unregulated in the world's largest AI market.

5. Privacy erosion and inference risks

AI systems benefit from more data — and this incentive directly conflicts with privacy. Your voice assistant conversations, health app data, and publicly posted photos are potentially part of training systems you have no practical ability to opt out of. More insidiously: AI can infer private information (health status, financial stress, sexual orientation, political views) from non-private behavioral data. These inferences may be wrong but are acted upon by algorithmic systems making consequential decisions about insurance, job applications, and loan approvals — without disclosure, without appeal, and without the subject knowing it happened.

6. The social fabric under stress

72% of US adults report significant stress about AI's impact on society (APA, 2024). Over 70% of US teens have tried AI companions; 1 in 3 find them as satisfying as real friendships. Research consistently shows heavy emotional self-disclosure to AI associated with lower well-being; people with fewer human relationships are more likely to seek AI companionship, creating a reinforcing cycle. 88% of higher education students now use AI for assignments; 94% of AI-generated work goes undetected by current tools. The credential inflation this creates — where degrees signal less about actual capability — is a systemic disruption of sorting mechanisms the labor market relies on.

Section 5 — Wild & Unexpected

Things that surprised even the experts

Transformative technologies routinely produce consequences their creators and critics didn't foresee. These are not edge cases or speculation — they are documented outcomes that nobody planned for. Their purpose here is epistemic: to build appropriate humility about predictions, including the ones in this report.

Unexpected
AI fraud built a polished criminal operation for $60/month
What used to require a criminal organization — convincing voice cloning, personalized scripts, scalable delivery — now costs approximately $60/month and can be built in hours. The Arup case ($25.6M lost to an all-deepfake video call) was the extreme. The norm is hundreds of thousands of voice-cloned emergency scams weekly. This was not in any AI risk forecast from 2022.
Unexpected
AI discovered antibiotics nobody was looking for
MIT researchers used AI to screen 36 million molecules against antibiotic-resistant bacteria. The AI identified candidates eliminating MRSA in mouse models. No new antibiotic classes had been discovered since 1987. This was not the goal — the goal was to test if AI could screen faster than traditional methods. The antibiotics emerged as a byproduct of pursuing that narrower question.
Unexpected
AI companions became de facto mental health infrastructure
Nobody designed AI companion apps to be primary social infrastructure for tens of millions of isolated, grieving, or disabled people. That's what they became. When Replika changed its terms of service in 2023, some users described genuine grief — a response the company had not anticipated and had no protocol for. The relationship between AI companionship and human psychological health is now a legitimate urgent research question.
Unexpected
AI helped decode languages that died 3,000 years ago
AI pattern recognition is processing fragmentary written records from ancient scripts — including Linear A (undeciphered Minoan) and pre-Columbian Polynesian texts — cross-referencing structural patterns against related languages. Linguists who worked for decades on these problems say AI has identified patterns they missed. Partial linguistic reconstruction is underway for languages with no living speakers. Nobody predicted AI archaeolinguistics.
Unexpected
Confident language is a warning sign, not reassurance
Research documenting that AI is 34% more likely to use confident language when generating incorrect information flipped the intuition people bring to technology. We expect confident signals to indicate quality. In AI, the opposite is often true: the fluency and certainty of AI prose is independent of accuracy. This requires a cognitive adjustment that users are still making, two years after it was documented.
Section 6 — Personal Navigation

A guide for the life stage you're actually in

AI anxiety is real — 72% of US adults report significant stress about AI's societal impact, and 40% of workers worry their skills will become obsolete within five years (SHRM, 2024). But the most useful response to anxiety is not reassurance. It is concrete, specific, calibrated guidance grounded in what's actually happening and what's actually within your control.

👨‍👩‍👧
Parents
College prep in the AI era
AI doesn't invalidate higher education — it raises the bar for what matters. Durable skills: learning how to learn, genuine domain expertise, judgment under uncertainty, and social intelligence. Ask of any program: what does this produce that AI can't do or can't verify? Use the Department of Education's College Scorecard to compare earnings by program. The question isn't "will AI take jobs?" — it's "which skills compound over a career regardless of AI?"
💼
Mid-Career Workers
The three-category audit
Map your job tasks into three categories: what AI does better (delegate it), what AI does comparably but needs your oversight (supervise it), what requires your hard-won human experience and judgment (invest here). Mid-career strategy: use AI for Categories 1 and 2 to free time for Category 3 — where your 10–20 years of accumulated context become more valuable, not less. This isn't about learning to prompt better. It's about identifying your irreplaceable contribution.
🎓
Students
Learn with AI, not from AI
88% of higher education students now use AI for assignments. The risk isn't using AI — it's letting AI do the cognitive work that builds your capability. Use AI as a tutor, reviewer, and feedback source; not as a ghostwriter. Understand why the good output is good — develop taste by doing, not just by accepting. Every school banning AI is banning a tool that will appear in every workplace you enter. Learn to use it critically.
🏡
Retirees
Tools and threats
AI as a personal tool: medical information in plain language before appointments, help understanding legal documents and contracts, learning new subjects at your own pace. Primary concern: AI-powered scams. Voice cloning requires only 3 seconds of audio and is operating at scale — "grandparent distress scams" are now highly convincing. Establish a family code word for genuine emergencies. The rule: if someone is pressuring you to act immediately and asking for money, stop and call them back on a number you already have.
🏪
Small Business Owners
The solo-practitioner superpower
AI gives small operations access to capabilities that previously required teams: marketing copy, document drafting, financial analysis, customer communications, customer service at scale. The competition — including your larger competitors — is already using this. The question isn't whether to engage with AI tools, but which ones, for which tasks, with what oversight. Start with one specific time-consuming task and see what changes. The barrier to entry is now extremely low.
📚
Educators
The transparency approach
AI-transparent assignments work better than bans. Require students to document how they used AI, what they changed, and why. Evaluate process and revision, not just final product. The cognitive work of writing develops reasoning — protect that. But also: the students in your class will work in environments where AI fluency is expected and evaluated. Both responsibilities are yours. The question isn't AI or no AI — it's how to use it in a way that builds, rather than bypasses, the underlying capability.

Managing AI anxiety: what actually works

Research on technology anxiety consistently shows that people who understand the technology they fear are less anxious than those who avoid it. The unknown is reliably more frightening than the known, even when the known is genuinely challenging. The antidote to AI anxiety is not reassurance — it is accurate information and specific next steps.

The Three-Question Check

When AI anxiety spikes: (1) Is this worry about something happening now, or a projected future? (2) Is this within my control or outside it? (3) What is one specific thing I could do in the next 30 days to reduce this risk? This reframes diffuse dread into targeted problem-solving — the psychological state where useful action actually happens. Most of the scenarios people fear are 3–10 years away and contingent on many things that can change, including your choices and collective political engagement.

Scam detection: red flags in the AI era

AI-powered scams are more personalized, more convincing, and faster-moving than anything that came before. The key red flags, in order of danger:

Urgent pressure to act immediately. Every high-quality scam creates urgency — AI has made urgency-creation cheap and convincing. Any contact pressuring you to act before thinking is a scam flag, regardless of how credible the source seems.

Gift card / wire transfer / crypto payment requests. Legitimate organizations don't ask for payment this way. Ever. Not the IRS. Not your bank. Not your family member in a foreign emergency.

Voice calls from "family" in distress. Voice cloning from 3 seconds of social media audio is real and in use at scale. Establish a family code word for genuine emergencies. If you receive a distress call, hang up and call back on a number you already have.

"AI tools" requiring upfront payment to access. The leading AI tools are free to start (Claude.ai, ChatGPT, Gemini). Products claiming special capabilities and requiring payment upfront are almost universally scams. Legitimate tools are described by reputable tech journalism, operated by identifiable companies, and transparent about what they do.

Section 7 — Policy & Power

The good news: we are in the early years of AI regulation — the rules being written now will shape AI development for decades, just as GDPR written in 2016 still governs digital privacy globally. The window for meaningful influence is open right now.

What you can actually do

Action 01
Vote on AI and ask candidates about it specifically
AI policy is on the ballot in every legislative election. Candidates' positions on AI accountability, worker protection, data rights, and algorithmic transparency are meaningful and often underdiscussed. Constituents asking substantive questions about AI signals to campaigns that it's an issue that wins votes. The most impactful policy window is the one that's open right now.
Action 02
Contact representatives on specific bills
Federal AI accountability legislation, deepfake disclosure bills, data privacy frameworks, and AI-in-hiring discrimination bills are all active in Congress. The most impactful individual political action in the US system remains constituent contact — especially from people who can describe a specific concern rather than general anxiety. One specific, articulate phone call is worth more than fifty form signatures.
Action 03
Support AI accountability organizations
EPIC (Electronic Privacy Information Center), AI Now Institute, Algorithmic Justice League, and EFF are doing the research, litigation, and advocacy that creates accountability. They are substantially underfunded relative to the companies they monitor. A $25/month donation to one of these organizations funds work that shapes AI policy. That's not hyperbole — these organizations have measurably influenced regulatory outcomes.
Action 04
Exercise consumer and worker pressure
Companies respond to what customers demand and workers organize around. Demanding AI transparency — "Was AI used in this hiring decision?" "Does this product train on my data?" — creates accountability incentives. Unions in entertainment, publishing, and journalism have successfully negotiated AI use policies in collective bargaining; the model is spreading. Individual workers can raise AI governance questions in workplaces even without unions.
Section 8 — Practical Tomorrow

What to do Monday morning — and beyond

Information without action tends to increase anxiety rather than reduce it. The most common response to a complex problem is doing nothing because every option feels inadequate. These steps are specific, concrete, and calibrated for different timelines — any one of them is better than paralysis.

This Week

First steps

  • Try one free AI tool on a real task you have this week: Claude.ai or ChatGPT (both free, no credit card required)
  • Establish a family code word for emergency verification — voice cloning scam protection, do this today
  • Search "AI tools for [your specific profession]" and read one credible article about what's actually being used
  • Tell one person in your life one specific thing from this report
30 Days

Building fluency

  • Use AI assistance on 5 real tasks and note what worked, what failed, and what surprised you
  • Review privacy settings on apps using AI: health apps, smart speakers, social media
  • Read one article per week from MIT Technology Review, The Verge, or your industry's trade publication on AI
  • Look up your Congressional representative's position on AI legislation at congress.gov
6 Months

Strategic positioning

  • Complete one structured AI course — Coursera's "AI For Everyone" (Andrew Ng) is designed specifically for non-technical audiences and is free to audit
  • Map your job's three task categories (what AI does, assists, can't replace)
  • Have one substantive conversation with your employer about AI in your specific workplace
  • Join or start a local conversation about AI — library, community group, neighborhood association
1 Year

Long-term integration

  • Identify 2–3 specific AI capabilities that give you measurable advantage in your work
  • Support one AI accountability organization through financial contribution or volunteering
  • Help one person in your life navigate a specific AI-related challenge — scam protection, career question, tool adoption
  • Revisit your career trajectory and make one concrete decision informed by AI trends

Methodology

This report synthesizes research from peer-reviewed scientific literature, regulatory documents, industry research, investigative journalism, and direct analysis of AI system capabilities conducted across 2022–2026. Specific statistical claims are sourced to identifiable publications, government reports, or documented industry data. Where projections about near-term developments are made, they are clearly labeled as estimates and distinguished from documented facts. The AI capabilities assessment reflects direct evaluation and broad review of published use-case research rather than benchmark scores alone, which are useful but not sufficient for characterizing real-world performance.

Policy analysis reflects official legislative and regulatory records supplemented by legal scholarship and regulatory body guidance documents. Labor market statistics draw from named organizations (OECD, McKinsey Global Institute, WEF, Goldman Sachs, Bureau of Labor Statistics) and are characterized with appropriate uncertainty. The scam and fraud statistics draw from FBI Internet Crime Reports, FTC Consumer Sentinel Network data, and documented industry research. Limitations: the pace of AI development means specific capability and deployment claims may be superseded within months of publication; this report reflects the state of knowledge as of April 30, 2026.
