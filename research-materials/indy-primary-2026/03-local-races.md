# Local Races — Indianapolis May 2026 Primary
*Marion County Sheriff, Clerk, Prosecutor, and Township Offices*

## Marion County Sheriff — Democratic Primary

**No Republican filed. Democratic primary winner will become next sheriff.**
Current sheriff Kerry Forestal is term-limited (running for SD-31).

The sheriff oversees 800+ employees, the county jail (~$140M budget), warrants, building security, and sex offender registry.

### Gregory Patrick (D)
- Age 61. Northside Indianapolis. Captain, Marion County Sheriff's Office.
- 40+ years law enforcement: Indiana State Police (1984 start); reserve MCSO deputy (2005); multiple roles including inmate booking, investigations, judicial protection, current role managing staff/inmates.

**Policy Proposals:**
- Deploy deputies as school resource officers
- Establish **civilian review board** to review sheriff department actions
- Expand mental health training and jail counseling
- "Transparency and fiscal responsibility" focus
- Would stop housing ICE detainees beyond required short-term holds

**Accountability Concern — Florida (2004):**
When Patrick ran for Okaloosa County (FL) Sheriff in 2004, the Florida Elections Commission found 17 "willful" violations of Florida campaign finance laws: four incorrect campaign treasurer reports and accepting five cash contributions exceeding Florida's $100 cash limit. He received a $100 civil penalty. Patrick says he learned from the experience (his first campaign); one case was a check accidentally deposited into the wrong account.

**Assessment:** These violations are from 22 years ago, were civil (not criminal), minor in scale, and from his very first campaign. His civilian review board proposal is a significant accountability commitment for this role.

---

### Kelvis Williams (D)
- Age 69. Pike Township. Deputy Chief and Executive Officer, MCSO. Heads Homeland Security Division (county building security). 40+ years law enforcement: Indiana State Police trooper (1978), rose through all ranks including counterterrorism oversight; joined MCSO 2009. FBI National Academy graduate. Northwestern School of Police Staff & Command. Bachelor's in criminal justice, Martin University.

**Policy Proposals:**
- Increase sex offender monitoring frequency (currently every other month)
- Expand jail programming and motivational speaker access
- De-escalation training
- Collaborate with faith leaders on youth mentorship
- Would stop housing ICE detainees beyond required short-term holds
- Increase employee pay

**Accountability Concern — False Endorsement Mailer:**
Williams distributed a campaign mailer featuring himself alongside five other Democratic candidates (Myla Eldridge, Ryan Mears, Kate Sweeney Bell, Joseph O'Connor, Faith James Kimbrough) with language reading "Your official Democratic team!" and an "Official candidates" stamp with a donkey logo.

- This **falsely implied Marion County Democratic Party endorsement** — the party stopped endorsing primary candidates in 2023
- When initially questioned, Williams **said he had permission from all five candidates** — which was false
- He later **retracted that claim** and apologized: "It is my campaign, it was my error, and it is not how I do business or lead my life"
- The other five candidates on the mailer publicly stated they did not approve it
- Opponent Patrick called it "tricks" and said "Democratic voters deserve the truth"

**Assessment:** The mailer incident involves a recent deception followed by an initial lie about having consent before recanting. For a voter prioritizing accountability, this recent dishonest behavior is more disqualifying than Patrick's 22-year-old civil violations.

## Marion County Circuit Court Clerk — Democratic Primary

**No Republican filed at primary. Winner faces Republican Robbin Stewart in November.**
The clerk administers elections and manages court records.

### Kate Sweeney Bell (D, Incumbent)
- Age 54. Elected 2022. Previously two terms as Marion County Recorder and five years as **Marion County Democratic Party chair**.

**Record:** Claims securing elections from interference, protecting voting rights. Plans: digitize campaign finance forms, upgrade election equipment, raised staff pay.

**Accountability Concerns:**
- Ran for clerk in 2022 while serving as Democratic Party chair — giving her power over the primary she was running in. Eventually resigned as party chair but only AFTER winning the primary.
- Faces allegations of lawsuits over election violations and accusations of flouting state election laws
- Opponent alleges she owes money to an attorney by court order
- Widely described as Democratic establishment insider maintaining the status quo

**Campaign Finance:** ~$40,000 cash on hand — 10x+ more than challengers. Donors include prominent Indianapolis Democrats and 2027 mayoral candidate Vop Osili.

---

### Karla López-Owens (D, Challenger)
- Age 35. Born Morelos, Mexico; immigrated as a young child; learned English helping translate for immigrant communities; became U.S. citizen at 18. Deputy prosecuting attorney and director of community outreach, Marion County Prosecutor's Office. Leadership in Indiana Latino Democratic Caucus; bilingual field organizer, Indiana Democratic Party.
- Previously ran for office twice (2020 council special caucus; 2022 SD-46) without success.

**Campaign Focus:**
- Marion County had **second-lowest voter turnout among Indiana counties** in November 2024 — she frames this as a clerk accountability issue
- Pledges 200 community events per year as clerk to boost civic participation
- Prioritizes increased access to court documents and government transparency
- Reform-oriented challenge to insider Democratic politics

**Campaign Finance:** Under $4,000 at year start — significantly under-resourced vs. Bell.

**Accountability:** Explicitly running as reform candidate against establishment. No personal controversies.

---

### Bobby Kern (D) — DECEASED
- CEO/founder of Hoosier Enquirer (online political publication)
- Died April 3, 2026. Name remains on printed ballot by order of Marion County Election Board.
- **Votes for Kern will NOT count toward a viable candidacy.**
- If Kern received the most votes, Indiana law would require the Democratic Party to select a replacement nominee through its own process.

## Marion County Prosecutor — Note Only

**Ryan Mears (D, Incumbent) — Unopposed in Democratic primary.**
- Won 2022 with 59%. $293,000+ cash on hand.
- Announced early 2025 focus on **public corruption cases** 
- His office has pursued statewide corruption cases including sheriff misconduct
- Will face **Philip Foust (R)** in November general election
- Foust: former deputy prosecutor (2015-2021), now Speedway clerk-treasurer; Harvard Law; ~$4,000 cash on hand

**No choice to make in primary.** Mears is the clear incumbent.

- **HD-94 (Indianapolis):** Rep. Cherrish Pryor (D Inc.) vs. André Sisk Sr. (D) — limited info on challenger
