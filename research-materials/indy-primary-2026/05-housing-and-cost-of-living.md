# Housing Crisis and Cost of Living — Indianapolis/Marion County
*Background research for voter guide*

## Housing Affordability Crisis

### Key Statistics (2024-2025 data)

**Rent Increases:**
- Average rent in Indianapolis metro rose nearly 50% since 2015, from $899 to ~$1,339
- Single-family home rents rose 40%+ from 2019 to 2025 (Marion County median: $1,625; metro: $1,800)
- Newly constructed units average 41% more than pre-2020 properties
- Two-bedroom new construction: $1,500+/month; three-bedroom: $2,000+/month

**Cost Burden:**
- 50% of Marion County renters are cost-burdened (30%+ of income on housing)
- 25% are severely cost-burdened (50%+ of income on housing)
- Vacancy rate: 3.9% in Marion County (2024) — 15-year low; well below healthy market rate

**The Shortfall:**
- Only 32 affordable units for every 100 extremely low-income renter households
- Deficit of approximately 49,000 units
- Metro area under-building affordable units by approximately 1,750/year
- Combined shortfall of more than 100,000 housing units since 2000

**Homeownership:**
- Median home price: ~$317,000 (October 2025, Redfin), up 5.5% year-over-year
- Marion County renter median household income: just under $43,000

**The Housing Wage Gap:**
- Wage needed to afford a two-bedroom apartment in Indianapolis-Carmel: **$25.94/hour**
- Indiana minimum wage: **$7.25/hour** (federal minimum; no state or local increase)
- Average Hoosier renter earns: $17.92/hour
- A minimum wage worker would need to work **122 hours per week** to afford a two-bedroom apartment

**Evictions (The Human Cost):**
- 116,000+ eviction filings in Marion County between 2021-2025
- 20,000+ in 2024 alone
- Nearly 19,000 in the first nine months of 2025
- **Indianapolis ranks 6th among 27 cities tracked by Eviction Lab** for highest eviction filing rates
- 2025 point-in-time count: 24% increase in chronic homelessness
- Challenger Destiny Wells (7th CD): "This happened on [Carson's] watch"

## What the City Has Done

**Vacant to Vibrant:** Used American Rescue Plan Act and CDBG funds to convert city-owned vacant lots into affordable housing. ~200 lots listed for development; 47+ homes planned in following years. City spent ~$20 million of its ~$420 million in ARP funds on affordable housing.

**Zoning reforms:** Accessory dwelling units (ADUs) allowed since 2015 via "Indy Rezone." Similar housing types can auto-replicate in neighborhoods.

**Down payment assistance:** ~$7.3 million provided to Marion County homebuyers in 2024 (through INHP and related programs).

**INHP (Indianapolis Neighborhood Housing Partnership):** Acquiring properties near transit lines for affordable multifamily development; financial education and homebuyer assistance.

---

## What Has NOT Been Done

**No rent stabilization:** Indiana does not permit rent control; the City-County Council has not enacted stabilization; landlords face no limit on rent increases.

**No meaningful eviction protections:** Indiana is one of only five states with no statute allowing tenants to withhold rent for habitability issues. The FHCCI has recommended a rent escrow program — not enacted.

**Source-of-income protection (HB 1578):** Bill to prohibit discrimination against voucher holders — pending at state level, not passed.

**Housing Choice Voucher:** Indianapolis Housing Agency paused processing all new rent increase requests for HCV units as of July 10, 2025 through at least Q2 FY2026, citing payment discrepancies and program instability.

**State preemption laws:** Indiana laws strip cities and towns of the ability to govern themselves on housing policy — candidates running for state senate (like Forestal in SD-31) who propose repealing these laws are addressing a structural barrier to local housing solutions.

## Construction Barriers

- Building materials costs rose 41% over five years
- Labor and materials now run $200,000-$300,000 per unit to build
- State requirement for two stairwells in apartment buildings adds 6-13% to construction costs
- Developer tax credit changes, rising interest rates, and zoning complexity constrain affordable development
- City officials acknowledge they lack subsidy funding to close the gap between what it costs to build and what low-income residents can afford

## Cost of Living — Indianapolis Overall

**Overall index:** Indianapolis sits around 90-92 (U.S. = 100) — about 8-10% cheaper than the national average overall. But housing is rapidly converging with national levels.

**Monthly estimates:**
- Single adult: ~$2,505/month
- Family of four: ~$5,516/month

**Breakdown vs. national average:**
- Groceries: ~1% below national average
- Utilities: ~6-9% below national average (~$182/month for energy) — but rising rapidly due to AES Indiana rate pressures
- Transportation: ~1% below national average; gas ~$3.36/gallon; public transit ~$60/month
- Housing: increasingly at or above national average for low-income residents

**Income gap:**
- Median household income Indianapolis: ~$66,629/year (2024) — below U.S. median of $80,610

## Property Taxes — A Growing 2026 Crisis

**Why this matters now:**

- For the January 1, 2025 assessment date, Indiana's DLGF removed a downward adjustment ("Verified Economic Multiplier") from property cost tables → wave of higher assessed values
- Marion County Assessor added nearly **$5.5 billion** in assessed value to commercial and industrial properties, and nearly **$2 billion** to apartment complexes
- Some Marion County homeowners have seen their property taxes **triple** after reassessments
- Tax bills reflecting 2025 assessment arriving in spring 2026
- Appeal deadline for 2025 assessed values: **June 15, 2026**
- A 10% homestead credit (max $300) and expanded supplemental deduction partially offset increases
- Another round of substantial increases already set for 2026 assessments (payable 2027)

**Political response:**
- Multiple SD-29 and SD-31 Republican candidates making property tax relief a central issue
- Gov. Mike Braun has called for property tax relief
- Some Democratic candidates also addressing property taxes (Hicks' cost-of-living relief act includes utility tax elimination)

## Utility Costs

**AES Indiana (Marion County):**
- Seeking rate increases of ~13.5% total ($21/month for typical customer) by 2027
- Already received 6% increase approval
- Parent company AES agreed to $33B acquisition by BlackRock-led consortium (March 2026)
- Private equity ownership raises concerns about future rate increases

**The data center connection:**
- Data center expansion drives infrastructure investment that gets passed to residential ratepayers
- NIPSCO (northern Indiana): bills rose $77/month per 1,000 kWh over two years; up 90%+ since 2016
- Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission investigating five utilities over complaint surges

## What Candidates Are Saying

**Destiny Wells (D, 7th CD):** Links private equity (BlackRock) buying up housing to price increases. Says housing affordability connects directly to crime. Wants to lower costs for families including healthcare, housing, utilities. Explicitly criticizes Carson for doing nothing about high eviction rates during his tenure.

**George Hornedo (D, 7th CD):** Publicly owned land development for affordable housing; housing trust fund to break down government silos. Notes "one in four kids here in Indy lives in poverty."

**Demetrice Hicks (D, SD-29):** "Cost-of-living relief act" — eliminate utility sales tax, consumer protections against utility cost-shifting, prescription drug access. Framing economic affordability as the defining issue.

**Kerry Forestal (D, SD-31):** First bill would target Indiana preemption statutes that strip cities of ability to set their own housing policies.

**Lasima Packett (D, SD-31):** Property tax caps for seniors, veterans, fixed income. Tenant protections and workforce housing expansion.

**Allissa Impink (D, SD-46):** Affordable housing expansion. Opposes criminalizing poverty. Expanding child care.

**Clif Marsiglio (D, SD-46):** Renter protections against displacement. Opposes corporate tax breaks draining local resources.

**Mike Delph (R, SD-29):** "Aggressive property tax relief" as Gov. Braun-endorsed priority.

**John Ruckelshaus (R, SD-29):** After canvassing 12,500+ doors: consistent affordability and crime concerns heard. Property tax relief that protects local governments and schools.

## The Political Accountability Question

Despite years of Democratic majority control of Indianapolis city government and Democratic representation in Congress (André Carson since 2008):
- Eviction rates remain among the highest in the nation
- Affordable housing deficit has grown to 100,000+ units
- No rent stabilization or eviction protections
- Utility bills rising faster than wages

This is the central tension in the 7th CD Democratic primary: Carson and his allies argue federal investment is helping; his challengers argue the district's working families are worse off after 18 years of the same representation.

**Key question for voters:** Is the housing/cost-of-living crisis in Indianapolis due to forces beyond local control, or is it partly a failure of political will and accountability?
