# Data Centers in Indianapolis — Local Context
*Background research for voter guide*

## The Data Center Boom

Indianapolis has become a major data center destination due to:
- Generous state tax incentives (lasting up to 50 years)
- Central location and existing power infrastructure
- LEAP District (Lebanon) anchored by $10B Meta campus and Eli Lilly manufacturing in Boone County (adjacent to Marion County)

At least **264 data center-related bills** were introduced statewide in 2026. At least three Indiana counties (White, Putnam, and other rural counties) have imposed local moratoriums.

## Active Indianapolis Projects

### 1. Sabey Data Centers — Decatur Township (APPROVED)
- **Developer:** Seattle-based Sabey Corp.
- **Scale:** $4 billion, 130-acre campus near Kentucky Avenue and Camby Road; ~1 million sq. ft.; 250MW capacity
- **Approval:** Metropolitan Development Commission (MDC) voted 6-2, March 18, 2026
- **Zoning bypass:** Land was already zoned industrial (Decatur Technology Park) → Sabey only needed a variance, not a full rezoning → **elected City-County Council had NO vote**
- **Tax abatement:** 50% property tax abatement requested. Site generated ~$16,000/year in taxes; Sabey projects $10M+ over a decade
- **Community opposition:** 1,400+ resident petition signatures (vs. 24 in support). Hundreds at November 2025 public meeting. Concerns: noise, energy demand, water consumption, no meaningful jobs, depressed property values.
- **Resident lawsuit:** Seven Decatur Township residents filed for judicial review April 17, 2026, arguing the MDC used a "disguised rezoning" to bypass council approval in violation of state law. Case active in Marion County Superior Court.
- **Key politicians:** Councilor Josh Bain (pro, sought community protections); MDC President John Dillon presided.

### 2. Metrobloks — Martindale-Brightwood (APPROVED)
- **Developer:** California-based Metrobloks LLC
- **Scale:** $500 million, nearly 14-acre project near 25th Street and Sherman Drive
- **Approval:** MDC approved 6-2, April 1, 2026; City-County Council gave final approval **May 4, 2026** (one day before this election)
- **Community:** Historically Black neighborhood with documented lead contamination, ongoing remediation, neighborhood's only grocery store nearby. Strong environmental justice organizing.
- **NAACP reversal:** Greater Indianapolis NAACP initially supported project (Feb. 9, 2026) then reversed course March 9, citing resident opposition and calling it "environmental racism"
- **Lead group:** "Protect Martindale-Brightwood"
- **Shooting incident:** On approximately April 6, 2026, someone fired 13 shots at the home of Councilor Ron Gibson (who supported the project) and left a "NO DATA CENTERS" note. Gibson and his 8-year-old son unharmed. FBI assisting IMPD. No arrest made. Gibson stood by pro-data-center position.
- **Moratorium attempt:** Night of May 4 approval, Councilor Jesse Brown (D, District 13, Democratic socialist) introduced non-binding resolution calling on MDC to pause high-impact data center approvals — it passed. His binding effort to force a full hearing on Metrobloks failed.
- **Developer claims:** AES Indiana contracts (not residential grid); closed-loop cooling (19,000-47,500 gallons); $2.5M-$20M community infrastructure commitment depending on tax abatement

### 3. DC Blox — Irvington/East Side (PENDING — hearing May 14, 2026)
- **Developer:** Georgia-based DC Blox
- **Scale:** $2 billion, three-building campus near South Kitley Avenue and Pennsy Trail (former Ford factory site / Thunderbird Commerce Center)
- **Hearing:** MDC public hearing May 14, 2026
- **Zoning note:** Because no specific zoning classification exists for data centers, DC Blox filed a variance petition — again bypassing full council review
- **Jobs:** 600 temporary construction jobs; only 35 permanent positions

### 4. Google — Franklin Township (WITHDRAWN)
- **Scale:** 468-acre, $1 billion project
- **Outcome:** Google withdrew rezoning petition September-October 2025, hours before a City-County Council vote, after at least 17 councilors said they planned to oppose it
- **Concerns raised:** Electricity and water consumption, limited permanent jobs

### 5. American Tower Corp. — Pike Township
- Petition withdrawn; may refile

## Government Position

**Mayor Joe Hogsett (D):** Says the administration "does not get involved with zoning decisions." However, Hogsett chairs the board of Indianapolis Economic Development Inc. (IndyEd), a nonprofit he established, which **sent a letter to the MDC supporting Metrobloks**. He declined to intervene when Martindale-Brightwood residents asked him to. As of May 1, 2026, weighing a fourth term.

**City-County Council:** Split. Passed final approval for Metrobloks May 4; Google project would have failed (17+ councilors opposing before withdrawal). Passed non-binding moratorium resolution May 4 via Councilor Jesse Brown.

**Councilor Jesse Brown:** Leading elected opponent; introduced moratorium resolution.

## Proposed Zoning Regulations (In Progress)

City released draft ordinance in late April 2026 that would:
- Create new SU-47 special-use zoning district requiring full rezoning and public hearings for ALL new data centers
- Set 65-decibel noise cap at property lines
- Require 200-foot buffers between data center buildings and residential zones, schools, churches
- Ban generator testing 5 p.m. to 7 a.m.
- Require "will-serve" letters from power and water utilities confirming capacity
- Require operations plans covering cooling, water discharge, noise, decommissioning

**Timeline:** MDC public hearing May 20; Council introduction June 1; committee review June 8; target final approval July 2026.

**Criticism (Citizens Action Coalition, Ben Inskeep):** Draft "fails to include meaningful protections to residents" while creating "a clear pathway for more data centers." Noise restrictions still too weak; still allows diesel generators.

## Power Grid and Utility Impacts

- **AES Indiana** (serving Marion County): Seeking rate increases totaling ~13.5% ($21/month for typical customer) by 2027, on top of 6% already approved. Parent company AES agreed to $33B acquisition by BlackRock-led consortium (announced March 2026).
- **NIPSCO** (northern Indiana): Bills rose $77/month per 1,000 kWh over two years; more than 90% since 2016. NIPSCO locked out 1,600 unionized workers April 2026.
- **IURC** investigating five utilities including NIPSCO over complaint surges. More than half of 2026 complaints received have been about NIPSCO.
- **Gov. Mike Braun:** Called for lower utility rates: "We can't take it anymore."
- Analysts directly link data center expansion to rising residential electricity costs.

## The LEAP District (Statewide Context)

The LEAP district in Boone County (Lebanon, just northwest of Indianapolis) is Indiana's largest data center hub — ~9,000-acre industrial park anchored by $10B Meta data center campus and Eli Lilly manufacturing.

**Water controversy:** A plan would have Citizens Energy Group divert up to 25 million gallons of water per day from sources including Eagle Creek Reservoir to serve the LEAP site, with treated industrial wastewater discharged back within Eagle Creek Park.

**Victory for opponents (May 1, 2026):** Lebanon Utilities announced the discharge would NOT occur within Eagle Creek Park — a partial win for opponents. The broader water diversion deal and data center expansion remain active concerns.

## The Federal Dimension

**AI Data Center Moratorium Act** (Sanders/AOC in the Senate and House):
- Would pause new data center construction pending federal AI regulation
- Supported by: Destiny Wells and George Hornedo in 7th CD primary; Allissa Impink and Sam Glynn in SD-46; Mike Delph in SD-29 Republican primary

## Who Has Opposed Data Centers (Political Accountability)

**Candidates with clear anti-data-center positions:**
- Mike Delph (R, SD-29): moratorium as first act
- Sam Glynn (D, SD-46): ban on new data centers entirely
- Allissa Impink (D, SD-46): moratorium pending ordinance
- Clif Marsiglio (D, SD-46): actual organizing experience opposing Metrobloks
- Destiny Wells (D, 7th CD): supports federal moratorium; lives near affected neighborhood
- George Hornedo (D, 7th CD): supports federal moratorium
- André Carson (D, 7th CD): NOW claims to support moratorium — converted only after primary challenge pressure; credibility questioned given AES/BlackRock PAC donations

**Elected officials who approved data centers despite opposition:**
- MDC majority (6-2 votes on both Sabey and Metrobloks)
- Indianapolis City-County Council majority (Metrobloks, May 4, 2026)
- Mayor Hogsett (indirect support via IndyEd letter)
