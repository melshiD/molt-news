# AI Timeline 2024–2026: How We Got Here
## Research for "AI For Everybody 2026" Talk

---

## The Narrative Arc: From Novelty to Infrastructure

The story of AI's arrival isn't a single moment — it's a compounding sequence where each development made the next one feel inevitable. Understanding the timeline helps audiences realize why *right now* feels different, not just in degree but in kind.

---

## 2022: The Spark

**November 30, 2022 — ChatGPT launches**
OpenAI releases ChatGPT to the public. Within 5 days, 1 million users. Within 2 months, 100 million — the fastest consumer adoption of any technology in history (faster than smartphones, social media, the internet itself). Most users' first reaction: "This is different."

The key thing ChatGPT did wasn't technical sophistication — it was *accessibility*. You talked to it in plain English. No special commands, no code, no manual. This was the moment AI became a *people thing*, not just a *researcher thing*.

---

## 2023: The Year Everyone Noticed

**Image generation goes mainstream**
Midjourney, DALL-E 3, and Stable Diffusion begin producing photorealistic images from text descriptions. Stock photo companies lose 20-30% of revenue. Professional photographers report first meaningful competition from AI. The phrase "AI-generated art" enters common usage.

**Coding assistants become standard**
GitHub Copilot surpasses 1 million paid subscribers. Developers report 30-50% productivity gains. The debate begins: are we raising the floor for beginner coders or threatening the jobs of senior ones? Both, it turns out.

**The "jailbreaking" culture**
The first year saw millions of people probing AI systems for their limits — trying to make them say forbidden things, game the system, find the edges. This became a global stress test for AI safety and exposed how alignment (making AI do what we want) remains unsolved.

**Voice cloning emerges as threat**
Within months of new voice synthesis tools launching, scammers begin using AI-cloned voices to impersonate family members in "grandparent scams" — calling elderly people pretending to be their grandchildren in distress. The FBI issues warnings. First major deepfake lawsuits filed.

---

## 2024: The Intelligence Inflection

**January 2024 — Claude 3 raises the bar**
Anthropic's Claude 3 Opus scores above human average on multiple professional benchmarks including bar exam, medical licensing, GRE. Not every task — but the curve is steep. For the first time, general-purpose AI is reliably *better than most people* at specific cognitive tasks.

**February 2024 — Context windows explode**
Google releases Gemini 1.5 with a 1-million token context window (roughly 750,000 words — the entire Harry Potter series is about 1 million words). AI can now read and reason across entire books, entire codebases, entire legal documents in a single session. Research workflows change overnight.

**May 2024 — GPT-4o: the multimodal moment**
OpenAI releases GPT-4o ("o" for omni) with seamless voice, vision, and text integration. In the demo, it reads a handwritten math problem, explains it step by step, and coaches someone through solving it — while maintaining natural conversation. The "Her" movie scenario feels less fictional.

**October 2024 — Reasoning models emerge**
OpenAI's o1 model introduces "chain of thought" reasoning — the AI thinks step by step before answering, dramatically improving performance on math, logic, and science. It's the first time AI systems reliably outperform PhD-level experts on specific hard problems. The "AI is just autocomplete" narrative becomes hard to defend.

**The election deepfake crisis**
The 2024 US election cycle sees an estimated 500,000+ pieces of AI-generated election content. Fake audio of candidates saying things they never said. Fake videos of ballot counting irregularities. The platforms struggled; fact-checkers were overwhelmed. A preview of information warfare at scale.

---

## 2025: The Agency Year

**January 2025 — DeepSeek R1: The Rules Change**
Chinese AI lab DeepSeek releases R1, an open-source reasoning model matching or exceeding GPT-o1 on benchmarks — reportedly built for a fraction of the cost using techniques that circumvent high-end chip requirements. NVIDIA stock drops 17% in a single day (~$600 billion in market cap). 

The significance: if the US chip export controls were meant to slow AI development elsewhere, DeepSeek was the proof they weren't working. And if a powerful model could be built this cheaply, the "only trillion-dollar companies can do this" narrative collapses.

**February 2025 — Claude 3.7 Sonnet with Extended Thinking**
Anthropic releases Claude 3.7 Sonnet with extended thinking capability — the ability to spend more "thinking time" on harder problems, visible to users as a stream of reasoning. First major commercially deployed "thinking model" from a safety-focused lab.

**Spring 2025 — Agentic AI becomes real**
The shift that defines 2025: AI stops being a chatbot and becomes an *agent* — software that takes actions, not just generates text. AI agents begin browsing the internet, writing and running code, sending emails, booking appointments, managing files, autonomously completing multi-step tasks. 

The difference is profound: before, you asked AI and got an answer. Now you give AI a goal and it figures out how to achieve it — including the intermediate steps you didn't think of. Early agentic systems from Anthropic (Claude), Google (Gemini), and OpenAI begin shipping to enterprise customers.

**GitHub reports: AI writes majority of new code**
By mid-2025, GitHub data shows AI-assisted code makes up the majority of new commits on the platform. Junior developer job postings drop measurably. Senior developers using AI report 3-5x productivity increases. The industry grapples with "what does a software engineer do now?"

**AlphaFold 3 and the biology revolution**
DeepMind's AlphaFold 3 expands protein structure prediction to all biological molecules — proteins, DNA, RNA, small molecules. Drug discovery timelines that previously took 5-10 years compress toward 1-3 years. Multiple pharmaceutical companies announce AI-designed drugs entering clinical trials.

**The autonomous AI moment in science**
Multiple published papers in 2025 list "AI" as a co-author for substantive contributions to research methodology, data analysis, and in some cases hypothesis generation. Nature and Science begin formal policies on AI disclosure.

---

## 2025-2026: The Structural Shift

**Labor market signals become undeniable**
By late 2025, the macroeconomic data that cautious economists had been hedging on begins arriving: job postings for certain categories — entry-level writing, basic data analysis, paralegal work, graphic design — decline measurably. Not catastrophically, but measurably. The BLS begins a new category: "AI-displaced."

**The power consolidation question**
Seven companies control the frontier AI systems most people use: OpenAI (Microsoft-backed), Google DeepMind, Anthropic (Amazon-backed), Meta AI, xAI (Elon Musk), Mistral, and DeepSeek. Combined market capitalization of the AI infrastructure layer crosses $3 trillion. Congress begins asking questions about monopoly.

**EU AI Act: Enforcement Begins**
The European Union's AI Act, passed in 2024, begins its enforcement phase for high-risk AI systems. Companies deploying AI in medical, legal, hiring, and educational contexts in Europe must demonstrate compliance, transparency, and human oversight. The US watches. Some companies launch "EU-compliant" and "US-standard" versions of the same product.

**The everyday normalization**
By April 2026, for many knowledge workers, AI assistance is as standard as spell-check was in 2005. Not everyone uses it. Not everyone uses it well. But the baseline assumption has shifted: "have you checked with AI?" is now a normal workplace question, in the same category as "have you Googled it?"

## The Central Narrative for Speakers

The story isn't "AI went from bad to good." It's: **we crossed from a world where AI was a curiosity to one where it is infrastructure.** Infrastructure is invisible, essential, and unevenly distributed. Everyone is affected whether they use it or not — because the people and institutions around them are using it.

That's the message that lands: you don't have to use a car to be affected by the automobile age. But it helps to understand how cars work, who owns the roads, and where you want to go.
